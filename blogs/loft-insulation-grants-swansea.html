Are There Grants Available for Loft Insulation in Swansea?

Information checked: 7 November 2026. Funding rules and application availability can change.

Yes. Swansea households may qualify for funded loft insulation through the Welsh Government’s Nest scheme or, where applications are still being accepted, the Energy Company Obligation scheme, ECO4. Neither route guarantees free insulation simply because a property is in Swansea. Household eligibility, the home’s energy performance and an assessment determine whether work can be offered.

The Great British Insulation Scheme ended on 31 March 2026. ECO4 has a different closing date: 31 December 2026. Older adverts often mention these schemes together, so the scheme name and current availability matter.

Which loft insulation funding routes apply in Swansea?

Nest is a Wales-wide programme providing free energy advice and, for eligible households, a tailored package of free home energy improvements. Insulation can form part of that package, but the assessment determines the measures offered. It is not a fixed cash allowance for every household to spend on its preferred work.

ECO4 is an obligation placed on participating energy suppliers to support energy-efficiency improvements. It can help with loft insulation, although qualifying for consideration does not mean a supplier must fund a particular installation.

Location-specific adverts can make a national programme sound like a separate Swansea council grant. Ask which scheme actually pays for the work and who makes the funding decision. An advert mentioning Swansea is not evidence of council approval.

Who may qualify for Nest?

Nest’s published criteria cover owner-occupiers and private tenants, rather than council or housing association tenants. The household must receive a qualifying means-tested benefit or meet the scheme’s low-income rules.

The home normally needs an Energy Performance Certificate rating of E or below. Homes rated D or below can qualify where someone has an eligible health condition. The current guidance also describes a reserve list for D-rated households without an eligible condition.

Income limits depend on household composition, so there is no single earnings figure that applies to everyone. Not receiving benefits does not automatically rule a household out. Nest checks the relevant income, property and health evidence before confirming eligibility.

Is ECO4 still accepting applications?

ECO4 closes on 31 December 2026, following an extension beyond its original March deadline. That extension does not guarantee that every supplier has remaining funding or time to accept new cases. Ask whether new applications are being assessed and whether proposed work can be completed within the scheme timetable.

Eligibility involves the household’s circumstances and the property’s energy rating. Some households may be considered through a council referral route known as ECO Flex. A referral is not funding approval: the supplier still decides whether it can support the property.

The GOV.UK guide to the Energy Company Obligation explains the main eligibility routes and how to approach an energy supplier or local council. Check Swansea Council’s current participation rather than assuming a referral route is open.

Does “free loft insulation” mean every cost is covered?

Under Nest, an approved package is provided free to eligible households. That does not mean every improvement a homeowner requests will be included. A grant-funded insulation installation and a boarded storage area, for example, are different pieces of work.

For ECO4, establish whether the proposed work is fully funded or requires a contribution. Ask for written details of the insulation, preparation, ventilation work and any exclusions before agreeing. Do not treat an initial eligibility check as confirmation that all costs are covered.

These routes generally arrange approved work rather than reimburse an installation already commissioned privately. Paying for insulation first and hoping to claim the money back afterwards can leave the household responsible for the whole bill.

Can existing insulation be topped up?

A loft does not have to be completely empty to need improvement. Existing insulation may be thin or uneven, but an assessor must decide whether a top-up is technically suitable and eligible under the funding route. There is no automatic entitlement based on insulation depth alone.

Dry, serviceable insulation may be retained, while damp material requires the cause of moisture to be investigated. Roof leaks, restricted ventilation and compressed insulation under boards can affect the proposed work. Adding another layer does not repair a leaking roof.

Loft insulation and loft boarding also serve different purposes. If boards are present, explain how the space is used so the assessment can account for access and the insulation beneath them.

What happens during an application?

  1. Initial checks: the scheme checks the address, tenure and household circumstances.
  2. Evidence: relevant benefit, income, ownership or health documents may be requested.
  3. Property assessment: an assessor examines the home and identifies suitable improvements.
  4. Written proposal: approved measures, permissions, costs and arrangements should be explained before installation.

For Nest, the published free advice number is 0808 808 2244, Monday to Friday, 9am to 6pm. Eligible households can then have an assessment arranged. Advice is also available if eligibility is uncertain. Nest’s advice service is separate from the business publishing this article.

Useful information to have ready includes the property address, any existing EPC, landlord details where relevant and a description of existing insulation. Describe what is known; there is no need to enter an unsafe loft to obtain measurements for an initial enquiry.

Can tenants apply?

Private tenants may qualify, but the landlord’s permission is needed before work proceeds. Discuss access and any proposed changes with the landlord rather than assuming a successful household eligibility check authorises alterations.

Nest’s improvement package does not cover council or housing association tenancies. Social tenants should ask their landlord about planned energy upgrades and whether another applicable funding route is available. Social housing is not excluded from every energy-efficiency programme simply because Nest uses different tenure rules.

How can a grant offer be checked?

Identify the funding scheme, supplier or delivery organisation, then verify the offer through independently obtained contact details. Be cautious if an advert still presents the Great British Insulation Scheme as open after March 2026, or promises approval without checking household and property circumstances.

Ask who will install the insulation, what paperwork will be supplied and who handles defects or complaints. Keep the assessment, written scope, any contribution agreement and completion documents. These establish what was approved and who is responsible if the finished work differs from the proposal.
